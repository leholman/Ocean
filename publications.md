A chronological selection of my research — each title links through to the paper. The full list lives on [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=Y-EsZZwAAAAJ).

**[Shifting seas: understanding deep-time human impacts on marine ecosystems](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2024.0026)** — *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 2025. I led the guest-editor team for this 16-article theme issue of the Royal Society's flagship biology journal; the opening article synthesises how humans have shaped marine ecosystems over thousands of years.

**[Ancient environmental DNA indicates limited human impact on marine biodiversity in pre-industrial Iceland](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2024.0031)** — *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 2025. Uses ancient eDNA from marine sediment cores spanning over 3,000 years to show limited human impact on marine biodiversity around Iceland before the industrial era.

**[Navigating past oceans: comparing metabarcoding and metagenomics of marine ancient sediment eDNA](https://doi.org/10.1111/1755-0998.14086)** — *Molecular Ecology Resources*, 2025. Benchmarks the two leading approaches for recovering ancient DNA from seabed sediments to reconstruct past ocean life.

**[Tracing early pastoralism in Central Europe using sedimentary ancient DNA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.08.047)** — *Current Biology*, 2024. Led by Giulia Zampirolo: uses ancient DNA preserved in sediments to track the early spread of herding across Central Europe.

**[ngsLCA: a fast, flexible toolkit for taxonomic profiling of metagenomic data](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.14006)** — *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*, 2022. A widely-used bioinformatics tool, led by Yucheng Wang, for assigning taxonomy to metagenomic and ancient-DNA datasets.

**[Managing human-mediated range shifts in marine non-native species](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2021.0025)** — *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 2022. Shows how eDNA can be used for temporal, spatial, and haplotype analyses of non-native marine species spread, and what that means for management.

**[How does eDNA decay affect metabarcoding experiments?](https://doi.org/10.1002/edn3.201)** — *Environmental DNA*, 2022. Shows how the breakdown of DNA in the environment shapes metabarcoding results, with direct consequences for how surveys are designed and interpreted.

**[Animals, protists and bacteria share marine biogeographic patterns](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-021-01439-7)** — *Nature Ecology & Evolution*, 2021. In a flagship Nature-family journal: uses eDNA across the tree of life to reveal that animals, protists and bacteria follow strikingly similar large-scale patterns across the ocean.

**[Detection of introduced and resident marine species using environmental DNA metabarcoding of sediment and water](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-47899-7)** — *Scientific Reports*, 2019. A foundational study showing that eDNA from sediment and water can detect both established and newly arrived marine species — a practical basis for biodiversity surveillance.

**[Optimising the detection of marine taxonomic richness using eDNA metabarcoding](https://doi.org/10.3897/mbmg.2.28963)** — *Metabarcoding and Metagenomics*, 2018. Led by Kristy Deiner: an influential early methods study on how filter material, pore size and extraction method shape what eDNA surveys can detect.
